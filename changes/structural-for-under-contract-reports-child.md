With `structural: "reject"`, a `for` or `if` written directly under a tag that has a contract is now reported as the structural construct, as it is everywhere else. Before, the contract looked through the construct (control flow is transparent to it) and rejected what it held first, so a `for` with a body that the parent does not allow reported the first child as `MX_TAG_NOT_ALLOWED` and never reported the `for`. The same happened for text, a dynamic tag, a child that appears once but sits in a loop (`MX_REPEATED_CHILD`), a required child that sits in an `if` (`MX_MISSING_CHILD`), and a tag with `parents` whose parent is the tag the construct stands in or the top level (`MX_PARENT_NOT_ALLOWED`). Under `structural: "reject"` none of these is reported for what a `for`, `if`, `else-if` or `else` holds: the construct is the one error, at the construct, and its message still ends with the backticked construct. A child the contract rejects with no construct around it is still reported; a child inside a construct is reported once the construct is gone. With `structural: "pass"` (the default) nothing changes: a contract still checks what a `for` or `if` holds.
